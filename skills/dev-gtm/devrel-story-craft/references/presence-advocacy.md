# Presence & Advocacy

Synthesized from the Developer-Facing Startup Alchemist GTM playbook (Adam Frankl / Alchemist Accelerator, 2024) and aligned with authentic developer influence practices.

**Core Rule:** Your presence must be authentic or devs will detect the marketing and ignore you. You build presence by consistently showing up in the places devs already are, sharing real knowledge (not features), and letting the story (validated by TAB) do the selling.

## The Non-Negotiable Authenticity Rules (from playbook extracts on pages 5-6 context)

- Show up 80% of the time even when you have "nothing to say." The act of being present in the community (comments, replies, office hours, open source issues) builds the trust that makes your story land when you do have something.
- Never end a piece of content with "contentless content" (e.g. "What do you think? Drop a comment!" with no substance). Every post, thread, or talk must deliver a complete, useful idea on its own.
- You are not the hero. Your content should make the reader the hero who learned something they can use today.
- Haters and skeptics are future partners. Engage them publicly and honestly. Their objections often surface the real villain or gaps in your evidence. Turning a public critic into a TAB member or customer is the highest form of social proof.
- Social proof comes from customers and peers, never from your own claims. "We are trusted by X" is weak. "Here's what @realdev said after using it on their production workload" is strong.

## The 3 Steps to Effective Advocacy

1. **Establish authority through consistent, non-sales knowledge sharing.** Answer hard technical questions in public. Write the deep-dive post on the exact problem your category solves (before your product is mentioned). This is how you become the person devs listen to.

2. **Amplify customer voices, not your roadmap.** When a customer (especially a TAB member) succeeds, your job is to give them the microphone: quote them, co-author the case study with them, let them speak on the podcast or at the meetup. You sell the category; they sell your specific solution.

3. **Use presence to recruit and run TAB continuously.** Every conversation is an opportunity to identify the next TAB member. The people who push back hardest on your ideas are often the best validators.

## Red Flags for Inauthentic Presence (counters for common shortcuts)

- Posting only when you have a launch or feature to promote.
- Using engagement bait ("like and subscribe if you hate integrations!").
- Writing "I built X in a weekend" threads that are actually lead gen without disclosing.
- Replying to every mention of your product with "have you tried our connectors?"
- Ghosting the community between releases.

If any of these appear in an audit, the presence plan must be rewritten to prioritize 80% non-promotional participation first.

## Signature Conversations (generate these)

Instead of generic "here is our new thing" posts, create signature conversations that embody the story:
- "The integration tax is growing faster than teams can hire. Here's the data from 14 TAB conversations."
- Thread that starts with a real dev's pain quote, names the villain, then shows (with evidence) what the victory looks like — and only at the end mentions the category your product lives in.

This reference ensures that after story + TAB, the presence you build actually amplifies trust instead of eroding it with hype.
