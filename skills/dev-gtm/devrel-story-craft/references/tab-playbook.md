# TAB Playbook

Synthesized from the Developer-Facing Startup Alchemist GTM playbook (Adam Frankl / Alchemist Accelerator, 2024).

**Purpose of a TAB:** A Technical Advisory Board is not a focus group or beta users list. It is your mechanism to become a trusted authority by having real experts validate (and improve) your story before you tell it publicly. TAB members become your first customers and the voices that sell the category for you.

## The 3 Core Principles

1. **Recruit for diversity of pain, not just fans or power users.** Include the skeptical mid-level dev at a scaling startup, the indie hacker who hates boilerplate, the platform engineer at a large company who has seen every integration attempt fail. Homogeneous TABs produce weak validation.

2. **Validate the story (pain + villain + victory) before any public artifact is written.** The story canvas must survive TAB scrutiny. Only after the TAB has poked holes and you have incorporated real quotes do you write the launch post, landing page, or newsletter.

3. **Use the TAB to create customers, not just collect feedback.** The right TAB members will become your reference customers. Their words ("I used to spend 3 weeks... now it's 10 minutes") become the evidence that replaces your puffery.

## The 3 Diagnostic Questions (use on every TAB call)

These are the exact questions that surface "the change that increases your customers’ pain points daily":

1. "What is the specific change happening in your projects or at your company right now that is making [describe the core pain, e.g. 'integrating new data sources'] take more time or become more risky every month?"

2. "Walk me through the last time you had to [do the painful thing]. What steps ate the most hours, and what would have happened to the project if you had skipped any of them?"

3. "If this root problem were solved so completely that you never thought about it again, what would you and your team actually do with those reclaimed weeks that you cannot prioritize today?"

Always ask follow-ups. Record exact quotes. The answers become the villain definition and the victory painting.

## TAB Recruitment Email Template (addresses RED baseline failure of solution-first emails)

Subject: [Pain area] is getting worse for teams like yours — 25 min call for your perspective

Hi [Name],

I'm researching why [specific daily pain, e.g. "adding reliable data integrations still costs teams multiple weeks even in 2026"] keeps compounding for developers shipping real products.

Your name came up as someone who has shipped [related work] recently. I'm not looking for feedback on a product yet. I want to understand the reality of the problem from people living it.

If you're open to a 25-minute call, I'll send a $150 research credit (or equivalent open source sponsorship) regardless of outcome. No pitch, no demo.

Would you be willing? Happy to work around your schedule.

Best,
[Your Name]

**Why this works (per playbook):** It leads with the pain the TAB member feels. It positions you as someone studying the problem (trusted authority), not selling. It sets expectation of no pitch. It offers real value for time.

## Call Structure (30 min max)

- 0-3 min: Thank them, restate that this is research, confirm recording for synthesis only.
- 3-18 min: Ask the 3 diagnostic questions + follow-ups. Let them talk 80% of the time. Do not mention your product.
- 18-25 min: Share the current draft of your pain + villain + victory canvas. Ask: "Where is this wrong or incomplete for your situation?" Capture the corrections verbatim.
- 25-30 min: "If we solved the villain as described, would this be something you'd want to try first? Why or why not?" (This plants the seed for them becoming a customer.)

## Post-Call Synthesis (required before writing any story)

For each TAB member, produce:
- Direct quotes for pain, villain, victory.
- What was missing or wrong in your canvas.
- Whether this person is a strong candidate for an early case study.

Update the story canvas with the synthesized evidence. Only then proceed to draft copy.

**Rule:** If you have not run at least 5-7 TAB calls with diverse personas and updated the canvas from their words, you are not allowed to publish a story. This is non-negotiable.

This playbook directly eliminates the baseline failures where subagents wrote generic recruitment emails, used weak questions, and planned to "synthesize after writing the story."
