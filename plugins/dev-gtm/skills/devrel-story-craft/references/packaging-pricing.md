# Packaging & Pricing for Devs

Synthesized from the Developer-Facing Startup Alchemist GTM playbook (Adam Frankl / Alchemist Accelerator, 2024).

**Core Principle:** For individual developers, the best value proposition is "eliminate wasted time." Packaging and pricing must make the magic (the thing that removes the villain's power) free or trivially accessible, while charging for scale, reliability, or team coordination that only matters after the dev has already won their first personal victory.

## The "Magic Should Be Free" Rule

The core transformation that makes the villain irrelevant must be available at $0 with zero friction. If the first success requires a credit card or sales call, you have failed the packaging test.

Examples of magic that must be free:
- The ability to try the exact integration in <5 lines in a sandbox with no signup wall.
- The core library or CLI that removes the boilerplate.
- Enough usage to reach first success (e.g. 1000 integrations or 30 days of real workload).

What you can charge for (after magic):
- Production SLAs, higher limits, SOC2, dedicated support.
- Team seats, audit logs, collaboration features.
- Hosted control plane when self-hosting becomes the new pain.

## Value Metric = Eliminate Wasted Time (Two Types)

There are two types of time that matter to devs:

1. **Time to first success (the hero's journey time).** This must trend toward zero. Your free tier and onboarding must be measured against this.
2. **Ongoing time tax (the villain's daily compound).** This is the recurring boilerplate, maintenance, and risk that grows with every new source or user. Your paid tiers should make this tax disappear or become someone else's problem.

Pricing conversations with prospects or TAB members must always tie back to one of these two time savings with specific numbers from their context.

## Win-Win Pricing Principles

- Transparent published pricing with no "contact sales" for anything a solo dev or small team would need to reach first success and early production.
- The free tier must be generous enough that a real developer can fall in love and start recommending it before they ever see a bill.
- Upsells must feel like relief, not a shakedown. "You've outgrown the limits that were protecting you from your old pain — here's the plan that removes the limit."
- Self-serve for everything until the customer explicitly asks for a contract (this is usually when they have already succeeded personally and now need to bring a team or compliance onboard).

## Transparency and Trust Rules

- Publish your packaging decision tree publicly (this reference + your own version).
- Never hide limits behind "enterprise only" if a solo dev doing real work would hit them on day 3.
- If you offer a hosted version, also make the self-hosted / open source path first-class. Many devs will only trust you after they have run it themselves.
- "Hosted as new open source" pattern: When a capability becomes table stakes, consider open sourcing the previous generation so the community can maintain the magic layer while you charge for the next layer of scale.

## Audit Checklist for Packaging & Pricing (use in Review mode)

- [ ] Can a developer reach a meaningful first success without entering a credit card or talking to a human?
- [ ] Does the free experience include the actual magic (the part that kills the villain) or only a crippled demo?
- [ ] Is every pricing claim ("saves 3 weeks", "10x faster") backed by a TAB quote or customer measurement?
- [ ] Would a skeptical dev on Hacker News call this pricing "honest" or "gotcha"?
- [ ] Does the packaging make the story (pain → villain → victory) visible in the UI and docs?

This reference closes the loop: a validated story + TAB-validated packaging means your presence can honestly invite devs into an experience that delivers on the promise without sales theater.
