# freeCodeCamp Integrity and Policy Rules

Canonical: [Publication Style Guide](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/developer-news-style-guide/) sections on plagiarism, AI, cross-posting, self-promotion, and finishing process.  
Related: `publication-style-guide.md`, `hashnode-workflow.md`.

These rules are **hard**. Violations can mean deletion, lifetime bans, or rejection — not soft style nits.

## Always credit sources — do not plagiarize

**Plagiarism** = presenting someone else's writing, images, code, and so on as your own. freeCodeCamp treats it with career-level seriousness. Documented cases: tutorials removed; authors banned for life.

Plagiarism is usually intentional, but accidental failure to cite or quote can still cross the line. When in doubt, cite.

### How to cite properly

When paraphrasing or quoting another tutorial, video, course, docs, Stack Overflow, GitHub, or similar:

1. Credit the source.
2. Link to the original.
3. Use pull-quote formatting for quoted material.

Example pattern:

> "This game is controlled entirely by typing into a command line interface. Because the game is real-time in nature, this can lead to some intense moments of rapidly typing commands as you try to save your drones from danger." ([Source: Quincy Larson](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/best-coding-games-online-adults-learn-to-code/))

**Official documentation, Stack Overflow, GitHub, and similar count** — copy/paste without citation is still plagiarism.

Attribute multi-line quotes to original speakers; pull quotes break up long paragraphs:

> "When you have wit of your own, it's a pleasure to credit other people for theirs."  
> ― Criss Jami

If code is heavily inspired by or borrowed from someone else, credit them.

Before publishing work that leans heavily on one source, ask: **Does this tutorial expand substantially on that work?** If not, it may not warrant a freeCodeCamp tutorial.

Prefer digesting and explaining in **your own words**. Excessive quote-stacking is a smell; when you must borrow, **cite**.

### Plagiarism examples (what not to do)

#### Example 1 — Verbatim sandwich (clear)

**Original (Abbey Rennemeyer):**

> Just a quick note before we get started: Instagram's desktop interface and mobile app are quite different. Most people use Instagram on their mobile device (from the Instagram app) because that's where you can actually post photos.

**Plagiarized pattern:** Original filler above and below, with the original paragraph inserted **unchanged and uncited** in the middle.

Lesson: sandwiching stolen prose between original sentences is still plagiarism without citation.

#### Example 2 — Close paraphrase (still plagiarism)

**Original themes:** many reasons to share photos/videos on Instagram (business, product, company presence, personal brand, sharing interests); Instagram is a great place to share ideas/messaging/art.

**Plagiarized pattern:** Nearly the same structure and ideas with light synonym swaps ("lots of reasons", "Insta", "organization", "create your own brand") and **no citation**.

Lesson: changing a few words while keeping the source's structure and content is still plagiarism.

If unsure what counts: research citation norms and create original work.

## Generative AI policy

Generative AI can help developers and writers. **It must not replace the author's contextual knowledge and skillful writing.**

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---------|-------------|
| Research assistance (ChatGPT/GPT-4, and so on) | Copy/paste an entire AI-written article into Hashnode with little/no modification |
| Help generating unique code samples | Rely on AI as the sole "knowledge" without fact-checking |
| Assistive drafting that the human rewrites and owns | Ship untested code samples |

Required practices:

- Write the tutorial yourself; AI is optional help.
- **Run and test all code samples**.
- Format code (for example Prettier) — no syntax errors in samples.
- **Fact-check extensively**; AI often errs.
- Quality over quantity — take time.

freeCodeCamp belief quoted in the guide: quality beats volume. (Beverly Sills: "There are no shortcuts to any place worth going.")

### AI-generated art

**Not allowed** in the community publication. Do not include. Editorial removes AI art and may save a copy for the author.

## Cross-posting

Cross-posting is generally **ineffective** and discouraged for open web syndication.

**Prefer:** publish the tutorial in **one** place — freeCodeCamp **or** own blog **or** a magazine — not all open channels.

**Exceptions:**

1. Cross-post inside a **walled garden not indexed by Google** (for example LinkedIn) can be OK.
2. Feature writing from other publications on a personal blog for employers using a **canonical URL** back to freeCodeCamp (reduces Google duplicate confusion).

Also allowed: anthologize personal posts on a similar topic into one longer freeCodeCamp tutorial.

**Philosophy:** freeCodeCamp invests coaching, meticulous editing, and community publicity — so do **not** cross-post the same piece to open sites like Medium.

## Acceptable self-promotion

freeCodeCamp.org is a **donor-supported public charity**. Paid placement impressions harm donation trust.

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---------|-------------|
| One-sentence CTA for a product/course/list/Twitter **at the end** | Opening the tutorial with a product link (spammy) |
| Affiliate links **only** for books/courses **you** created | Other affiliate spam |
| Tasteful promotion | Branded accounts; ghostwriting; transferring tutorials between employees |
| | Writing stories on behalf of people who have **not** earned a contributor account |

SEO note: links on the publication are `rel="doFollow"` (unlike many sites). Each outbound link can pass ranking signal — **do not overdo** self-links.

## Paid / sponsored writing disclosure

If a company **pays** the author to write an article and publish on freeCodeCamp's community publication, **disclose this to editorial when submitting the draft**.

## G-rated and Code of Conduct

- Avoid profanity unless quoting.
- Avoid offensive memes.
- Code of Conduct violations → immediate deletion (copy retained for author records).

## Images (integrity-adjacent)

- Own creations OK; otherwise no-attribution stock (Pexels, Unsplash, Wikipedia).
- No hotlinking; upload to Hashnode; prefer <1MB.
- Alt text required for meaningful images.
- No AI art (above).

## Finish process (integrity of submission)

When confident the story is ready:

1. Hashnode **Submit** under freeCodeCamp publication settings.
2. Email draft link to **editorial@freecodecamp.org**.

Editorial may improve headline, opening, formatting, grammar. Further work → email request → author revises → re-notify.

Both steps required — see `hashnode-workflow.md`.

## Reviewer quick flags (integrity)

Mark **FAIL** (block publish) for:

- Uncited verbatim or close paraphrase
- Unmodified AI article body
- AI-generated cover/inline art
- Hotlinked images or missing critical alt text when images teach
- Cross-post plan to Medium/open sites without exception path
- Product pitch in the opening
- Undisclosed paid placement (when known)
- Ghostwriting / branded account indicators
- Dual-submit incomplete (submit without email or email without submit)

Mark **WARN** for:

- Thin expansion of a single source (borderline originality)
- Heavy quote density without enough original teaching
- Self-links approaching spam density
- Affiliate-looking links that need clarification
